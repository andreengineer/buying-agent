# Product — Family cohort #1

## Problem (as observed, not assumed)
In most families one tech-literate person ends up researching purchases for everyone else.
That person is the bottleneck, and the people they help are the most exposed to bad sellers.
The cohort tests whether an agent can take that role for real requests in one week.

Link to the main thesis: a family member delegating a purchase is the same primitive as a
UHNW principal delegating to a house manager — **delegated spend authority with an audit trail**.
The autonomy ladder (A0→A3) is the product, not a safety add-on.

## Value hypotheses (each must produce a number by Sunday)

| # | Hypothesis | Seed evidence | Tag | Metric this week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Research value concentrates in durables, not groceries | Same-model phones varied 79–97% and a robot vacuum 75% across online stores in Vitória [1]; online supermarkets vary up to ~200% on single items [2] but basket-level gaps are far smaller (up to ~21% a year in one city survey) [3] | [Likely] | median savings vs reference, by category |
| V2 | Avoiding bad sellers is worth as much as finding low prices | ~45% of 2025 digital-fraud reports involved purchases, mostly fake stores imitating large retailers [4]; 2.7M online-purchase frauds counted in 2025 [5] | [Likely] | share of cheapest offers excluded by assurance checks |
| V3 | The chat summary (L2) is enough for most decisions; the report (L3) builds trust for high tickets | Beer-fridge run: the PDF scorecard was the artifact that landed [stated by André] | [Guessing] | L3 open rate vs ticket size |
| V4 | Requesters accept "agent prepares, human pays" today | Card networks ran their first fully agent-made payments in Brazil in March 2026 [6]; the first agentic Pix MCP keeps the agent from moving money alone [7] | [Likely] | A1 acceptance rate |
| V5 | Pix is the right rail for A2 later | Pix reached 44% of online checkout in 2026 vs 41% for cards [7] | [Certain] for share · [Guessing] for agent fit | research card H5 |

Caveats: Procon surveys exclude shipping [1] and pick extreme items, so they overstate typical
savings. That is why AC-3 uses landed cost and the reference is the median valid offer.

## Metrics (week 1)
| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Real requests answered with L2 + L3 | ≥ 3 | proves the loop end-to-end |
| Purchases (A0 or A1) | ≥ 1 | proves the decision step |
| Median time complete-request → L2 | ≤ 10 min | convenience bar |
| Median savings vs reference landed cost | measured, no target | V1, pricing input |
| Mean rating | ≥ 4.0 | trust |
| Duplicate replies | 0 | reliability |

## Monetization tests (buy-side only — settled)
Run as questions to the cohort in week 2, after real value is measured. No charging in week 1.

| Model | Shape | Fits when |
|---|---|---|
| Savings share | 10–20% of measured savings vs reference | V1 holds with a credible reference price |
| Per-report fee | flat fee per L3 report | V3 holds for high tickets |
| Household subscription | monthly, unlimited requests | frequency > 4 requests/month |

Affiliate and cashback are excluded: they are supplier-side revenue and conflict with the
buy-side positioning. Research may record them only as foregone revenue.
